Data Mining —
Scaling to Large Data

Dr. Innocent Nyalala (Weeks 1–6) Sept 14 – Oct 30, 2026 6 Weeks · 3 hrs/week BS Programme
This course runs for 15 weeks total. Dr. Nyalala teaches Weeks 1–6 (foundations). A second instructor covers Weeks 7–15. This page covers Weeks 1–6 only.

Course Overview

What This Course Is About

How do you mine data when it is too large to fit in memory — or on a single machine? This course covers the algorithmic ideas behind Google, Amazon, Netflix, and Shazam: MapReduce, locality-sensitive hashing, stream mining, PageRank, frequent itemsets, and recommendation at scale.

Core Textbook

Mining of Massive Datasets
Leskovec, Rajaraman & Ullman
Cambridge University Press
Free PDF — mmds.org ↗

Prerequisites

Basic probability and linear algebra. Familiarity with Python. Some exposure to algorithms or databases is helpful but not required.

Lab Environment

All labs run in Google Colab (free). PySpark is used from Week 1 onward. You only need a Google account — no local installation.

What Makes Each Week Interesting

Week 1 — MapReduceGoogle had 10 billion web pages and one deadline. They invented a new way to compute. Demo live: same word count, for-loop vs Spark — watch the time collapse.
Week 2 — LSHShazam identifies a song from a 5-second noisy clip. Same math as Google's near-duplicate web page detector. Students find near-duplicates in a real news dataset.
Week 3 — StreamsYour fraud detection system sees 10,000 transactions per second. You cannot store them all. You must decide in milliseconds. How? One-pass algorithms.
Week 4 — PageRankAssign the original 1998 Google paper (9 pages, free). Students compute PageRank by hand on a 5-node graph, then run power iteration on a real SNAP network.
Week 5 — Frequent ItemsetsAmazon's "customers who bought X also bought Y" runs across billions of transactions. Students mine real association rules from the Instacart grocery dataset (3M orders, free on Kaggle).
Week 6 — RecommendersNetflix offered $1,000,000 to anyone who could beat their algorithm by 10%. Students build a collaborative filter on MovieLens and see exactly why it is hard.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain why standard algorithms fail at large scale and describe the MapReduce/Spark computation model.
  • Apply shingling, MinHash, and Locality-Sensitive Hashing to find approximate nearest neighbours in large datasets.
  • Implement one-pass streaming algorithms (Bloom filter, Flajolet-Martin, DGIM) for data that cannot be stored in full.
  • Compute PageRank using power iteration and explain how it is adapted for spam resistance and topic sensitivity.
  • Use A-Priori and SON algorithms to mine frequent itemsets from distributed transaction databases.
  • Build a collaborative-filtering recommender and apply SVD / CUR decomposition for dimensionality reduction at scale.
